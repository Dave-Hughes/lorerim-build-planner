## Planner gaps found re-planning Kyria's Acts III and IV, 23 Oct 2026

Done by hand in the Book (v40–41) because the planner couldn't. Each item is something the tool should have answered.

10. **`suggest_quests` doesn't accept hold names.** `where: "Haafingar"` returned nothing; `where: "Solitude"` returned six lines. Holds should work (and Hjaalmarch, the Pale, the Reach).
11. **`suggest_quests` results often have `start: null`** (Bards College, The Wolf Queen Awakened, Lights Out!, Battle of the Champions, Odd jobs in Haafingar) even though `get_quest` on the same line returns a checked start. Fill it, or say why it's empty.
12. **`suggest_places` only works on the planner's own stored acts, and the stored playthrough is out of date.** Kyria's planner copy still has the old outline (Act II "The Bard's Road, 6 to 8"; Act III "The Knight Returns, 20 to 35"), so there was no way to ask for places near Solitude, Morthal or Dawnstar. Needs either a `where`/hold input or a sync from the Book's acts. Also: "The places index doesn't measure distances from The Reach yet", so Markarth's whole act had no place data.
13. **No enemy levels for the places the plan actually uses.** Fort Hraggstad, Frostmere Crypt (the Pale Lady), Movarth's Lair, Broken Tower Redoubt, Korvanjund, Nightcaller Temple's last room: none came back from the planner. Wolfskull Cave's 50 boss and Nightcaller's 19–32 Orcs came from the project's old placed-actor notes, not the tool. An act can't be levelled without these.
14. **`get_quest` returns no XP and no quest type.** XP had to be read from `src/data/.../quests.json` by hand. It should come back with the quest (type → XP from Experience.ini), and flag type-None quests that pay 0, such as **The Heart of Dibella** and **The Blessings of Nature**, which the Book had treated as XP payoffs.
15. **No XP-to-level data.** `get_rules` covers skill points and perks but not XP per level. The curve (25 × (level + 11) on Kyria's save, i.e. 575 at 12, 650 at 15) should be a rule the planner states and uses.
16. **Tome Trials is invisible, and its gate was wrong in our own notes.** The exams aren't quests, so the planner doesn't list them. In the files, Giraud's line offering exams checks `GetQuestCompleted` on Skyrim.esm:053511 (**MS05, Tending the Flames**) == 1, so all 36 exams wait for Act VII. `systems/magic.md` and the Act III plan treated them as open; `doc50` had it right. The planner should model Tome Trials (36 books, 50/100/200 XP, tier 2 Speech 50, tier 3 Speech 75 per the mod page, plus the Callings Historian half again) with that gate.
17. **Laid to Rest's start reads as a key/door condition** ("The door from Stonehills into Sorli's House needs the Key to Sorli's House…"), so `openForYou` is "unknown" for a quest that is already in Kyria's journal. Looks like a misread of the start node. It matters: Laid to Rest is one of Dawnguard's two gates.
18. **The save connector's snapshot has empty `skills`.** Speech gates Tome Trials tiers and follower slots; Two-Handed and Heavy Armor decide what an act can fight. The connector should read actor values.
19. **Missives: route-aware, never a grind line.** Dave's point: a missive picked for XP can send the player to Solstheim and overwrite the story. What the files say (Missives.esp, checked 23 Oct): every delivery note on a board is named **"Missive: Deliver Letter / Potion / Weapon to <Destination>"** and its text says "to <Recipient> of <Destination>"; only the journal title says Near / Medium / Distant. The destination is filled from a few candidate locations when the board refreshes (the dosemetha log shows "found 6 candidate locations" for a Haafingar letter), so no tool can predict it; Near/Medium/Distant is the distance band from that board's hold. Two uses the planner should support:
    - **Nearby:** at the board the player is standing at, Near deliveries and Heal the Sick that finish in town or close by.
    - **On the way out:** when an act's route moves the player from A to B, remind them to read A's board for notes addressed to B (leaving Markarth for Solitude, say).
    - Rule: never budget missives as a lump. Count only what the route can carry (one or two per leg), and never recommend one that leads off the route. Skip gathering jobs (LoreRim's author warns they can break).
20. **Gate chains across acts aren't surfaced.** Dawnguard needs level 30 and Laid to Rest; VIGILANT needs 25, The House of Horrors and Kindred Judgment. The Book's Act IV had no Laid to Rest at all. The planner should check that every later act's gates are reachable from the earlier acts' contents.

42. **`get_style_guide` has no rules for Tale prose, and `write_chapter` doesn't check any (Dave, 24 Oct 2026, reading Act III of the Kyria Book).** Rule 1 ("plain sentences that state a fact") is aimed at Play text and builds. The story chapters came out in the machine-made style the guide warns about, and Dave could not tell what several sentences meant. Add a Tale section to the guide, and a lint to `write_chapter` that returns warnings (not refusals) for the patterns below.

   **Rules to add to the guide (for rule 12's Tale):**
   - T1. Every sentence gives the reader a fact, an action or a reaction. If it can be cut and nothing is lost, cut it.
   - T2. No "X, not Y" or "so much as" framing. Say what she did. Bad: "She did not go looking for the armor so much as stop refusing it." Bad: "well enough to be asked back, not well enough to be remembered."
   - T3. No closing line that sums up the scene, and no one-sentence paragraph used for effect. Bad: "Dawnstar slept that night. So did she." Bad: "A soldier could walk the coast road with a reason. A bard could only walk it with a song." End the scene on the last thing that happens.
   - T4. No similes for a feeling the reader can't picture. Bad: "The weight settled onto her shoulders like a hand she knew." Name the feeling or the action, once.
   - T5. No coy narration that hints and withholds. Bad: "Some of it she found and some of it she paid for; she never said which." Say it or leave it out.
   - T6. Nothing the reader hasn't been told. A chapter may use a name, song, object or past event only if an earlier chapter or the same one introduced it with who or what it is. Bad: "I knew her once", "the other one" (a song never named), "the way Ottavian had taught her", "Mr Dragonfly". Test: someone who has read only the chapters before this one understands every noun.
   - T7. No refrains. Bad: "Nobody hummed four chords" used three times in two scenes. Say a thing once (guide rule 8).
   - T8. No stock gestures. Bad: wiping the same spot on the bar, sitting "for a long time", "a long while", "without lighting the candle", a pause before an answer. Use a detail the game has (a named room, an item, a price) or no detail.
   - T9. No hinting at what the game data doesn't give. A hook such as a witness, a ship or a lead may appear in the Tale only when the planner has it or the player supplied it; label a plant as the player's own (see 41). Every Tale event should have a Play step that makes it happen.
   - T10. Plain past tense, named people, the place named when it changes. Paragraphs of two to five sentences, each adding something new.
   - T11. No em dashes, no semicolons used to join two statements for rhythm, no lists of three.
   - T12. Before submitting, read each sentence and ask "what does the reader know now that they didn't?" Delete the ones where the answer is nothing.

   **Lint for `write_chapter` (warn, list the sentences):** "not ... but", "so much as", "a long while", "for a long time", "without a word", "as if", "like a" (similes, review), a paragraph of one sentence under 8 words, any sentence repeated verbatim or with 4+ shared words in the same chapter, a name that first appears in the chapter without a role (compare with names in earlier chapters and the planner's data), semicolons, em dashes.

   **Example rewrite (Act III, The Third Cove):** the draft had "The colours on her shoulder were reason enough to be on the coast road. She wrote down what she had seen, the way Ottavian had taught her, and put the page away. A soldier could follow a coast for as long as there were letters to carry along it." Better: "She was wearing Legion colours, so no one on the coast road would ask what she was doing. She wrote in her notebook that the boat had gone west." (Only if a notebook has been introduced; the check in T6 would catch it if it hadn't.)

43. **A reading view that interleaves the story with the steps (Dave, 24 Oct 2026).** Reading only the Tale left gaps, because many story beats come from quests. Give each act a "read it as one story" view: the route's stops in order, each with its quest steps as plain one-line beats and its chapters in between, so the player can read the act start to finish and see where the thread is missing. Dave's structure question: the three layers (prologue story, act tale, quest beats) should be two kinds of thing, chapters and steps. A chapter is Prologue or act and may unlock on a step, a level or a place, so nothing floats free of the game. Steps are the beats. The interleaved view is a rendering of those two, not a third store.
44. **Gap check found in Act III (24 Oct 2026, by reading the chapters and steps together).** Without the check, the planner would have shipped: no chapter for the stretch from the cove to the Blue Palace (Morthal, Dawnstar, the road back), the letter from Maramal never delivered, the College shifts with no reason, and nothing saying why she goes back to Solitude. Add to item 33: for each act, every stop with steps needs a chapter or an explicit "no story here", and the last stop needs a chapter that names the reason for the next act.

45. **Character first, quests second (Dave, 24 Oct 2026; this is the heart of the planner).** The MCP should drive this order: the player's brief (as little or as much as they give) -> the planner offers choices for whatever is missing -> backstory and Prologue -> what she wants, what she fears, the line she won't cross, her god, her traits and start -> the arc split into acts, each act a want and an obstacle -> only then the quests and tasks. An objective is admitted only if it (a) serves the act's want, (b) expresses who she is (god, traits, values), or (c) pays something the plan needs (XP, a gate, a start). The objective says which of the three. If none, drop it or mark it optional. Kyria's Act III failed this for Morthal and Dawnstar until the bridge scenes were added.
46. **Each objective carries a line for the character, apart from the game facts.** "Why Kyria cares": one or two sentences in the story's voice, no numbers. The existing why line keeps the game facts. Missing in Act III for the Pale Lady, Fists of Fury, Folgunthur and No News.
47. **The reading unit is the beat, so the player doesn't flip between story and log.** Setup (two to four sentences that end on the thing to do), do (one plain instruction, the objective), aftermath (written after play, from the save). Rules: no objective without a setup line, and no long run of story before the next thing to do. Item 43's interleaved view is the rendering of this.
48. **The save is a hint, not a verdict (Dave).** Players pick up quests by walking into a house and drop others. The planner reads the save for state and ticks what is done, but never calls the plan wrong because the journal differs from it. It flags only what blocks: a gate the route needs, or a step whose prerequisite hasn't happened. This replaces the "reconcile the plan with the save" idea in the 24 Oct review.
49. **Choice points tell the player what to do (Dave).** When a quest has endings (arrest or talk down, spare or kill, keep or give up an item), the plan says which one the character takes and why, in her terms, and names the other as the alternative. Lights Out! and The Taste of Death gave no recommendation, so Dave chose without guidance.
50. **Continuity lives on the server, not in the agent (answer to Dave's hosting question).** Players bring their own agent, so the planner can't control its memory or context. Keep a story bible on the playthrough: names with roles, the timeline, songs, what she knows, what the reader has been told. `write_chapter` hands back the bible and the last paragraph of the previous chapter before it accepts text, and rejects text that uses a name the bible doesn't hold. This works with any agent and any context size, so no hosted agent is needed.

51. **Correction to item 42: the prose rules over-corrected, and the story came out thin (Dave, 24 Oct 2026, reading the Play view).** Rules T1 to T12 removed machine-made atmosphere and also removed the experience. Dave: no arrival in Solitude, no execution in the square, no temple, no first day at the Bards College, no grotto raid with twenty dead, no clearing a whole fort. Fix to the guide: every scene must include concrete detail the player will see or do in the game (the place, the event, what Kyria does) and one plain statement of how she feels about it. Test for a sentence: could it only be about this place or this event? If yes it stays. What is banned is generic atmosphere, not description. Keep each scene short.
52. **Write the story around the game's events, not around the invented plot (Dave).** The Kyria plot (Pia, the man with rings) was the spine, and the things the player actually does were treated as errands. Reverse it. For each stop the planner lists the game events the player will meet (arrive, an execution scene, a dungeon clear with a count, a fort, a quest ending). The agent writes a short passage for each, in the character's voice and coloured by her thread. The planner holds the events (item 28, the quest, location and NPC data now being finished), and the agent supplies the reaction, so little needs inventing.
53. **A reason to travel must be on the page where the player travels (Dave).** Morthal had no reason for six drafts because the reason lived in a scene the player might not have read. Every setup for a new town states why she goes there next. Also: scene titles and terms must make sense to a reader who skipped the earlier scenes ("The Third Cove" meant nothing). Check each title and each proper noun in a chapter against the chapters before it and the setup text.
54. **XP is part of the plan, and it depends on the character (Dave).** Players cannot live on story and a few quests. At each stop the plan should say what pays XP for this character: a bard and scholar reads books and plays inns (about 340 XP a day at their limits); a hunter or wanderer needs different tasks (hunts, camps, travel discoveries). The planner builds these from the character's callings and class and puts them beside the story, with the act's XP gap shown (items 6 to 8). The Book's Act III only mentioned them in one task and had none at the College.
55. **Open design question (Dave):** the balance between story depth, gameplay instruction and the player's reading load has no known right answer yet. Ship the beat format (setup, do, what happened), test it on a few characters of different types, and tune the length of each part with players, not by rule.

56. **Scene weight (Dave, 24 Oct 2026; tried on Act III).** Each event gets a weight: heavy (changes her: the execution, the first big killing, the oath), medium (echoes her theme or the thread), light (routine work: shifts, songs, fist-fights, missives). Heavy events get a full passage with her reaction. Light events get no story and stay in the task list. Combine events that happen close together into one scene (Act III's grotto and lighthouse; the fort, the oath and the empty cove). The thread picks what is told: a scene exists only if it moves Pia's trail or tests the character's vow. State once, early, how she comes back from violence (Kyria plays the lute; Ottavian made her play scales for a year), so the next-day inn needs no explaining. Act III went from ten scenes to eight. Store the weight on each scene's unlock so the planner can check that no act has all heavy or all light scenes.
57. **Fallback if scene weights don't hold (Dave):** each act gets an intro (sets the scene, few spoilers) and a conclusion (what happened, what comes next), and the task list sits between them, with no per-event scenes.

58. **Act structure: intro, tasks with flavor, conclusion (tested on Act III, 25 Oct).** Story lives in two places only. The intro sets the scene and thread with no big spoilers and may run long. The conclusion is where the weight goes (killings, the lute afterward, the follower's comfort), told afterward, and is shown collapsed as "How the act ends (spoilers)". Between them there are no scenes: quests and tasks carry one or two lines of "why Kyria cares" (`flavor`), then the game facts. Add XP pointers that fit the calling, missives only where the route fits, and an "Also nearby" list of quests and places with no tie to the character, sourced from `suggest_quests` and `suggest_places` so nothing is invented. Reason: a scene between tasks is too small a unit to carry weight, and it makes the jump to the next task jarring. This replaces the scene-per-beat design (items 47, 56) as the default. Heavy-scene reveals can come back later if wanted.
59. **Act-end prompt: the player's agent turns a predicted conclusion into a recorded one.** The conclusion is written in advance and is labelled a prediction. At the end of each act the Book shows a copy/paste prompt. It carries the playthrough and act, a few act-specific questions (is the follower still with you, what happened at the heavy moments, how did the character feel), and a list built from what the player ticked, set aside or skipped. The agent asks the questions one at a time, rewrites the act's conclusion and the next act's intro from the answers, and shows both for approval before saving. The planner side needs: a tool that saves a conclusion and an intro per act, `agent_questions` stored on the act, and the style guide and story bible (item 50) returned to the agent so the rewrite keeps names and prose rules. It runs on the player's own agent, so there is no hosting cost.
60. **Check kill and crime rules before suggesting a story that depends on them.** Example: killing the Companions. Aela, Farkas, Vilkas, Njada, Athis, Torvar and Ria are Protected (the player can kill them, enemies cannot). The Companions' own crime faction (`CompanionsFaction`) has murder 0 and assault 0 and is flagged "do not report crimes against members", so no murder bounty is expected (read from records, not tested in game). Other Whiterun residents are normal murders at 10,000. The planner should expose NPC flags and crime-faction values so an agent can answer "can I kill them, and what does it cost" without a file scan.
